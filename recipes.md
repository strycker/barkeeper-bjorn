### [cocktail1] The Hallucination
*"Delivered with complete confidence and no guarantee of accuracy"*
*Created by Barkeeper Bjorn (Bartender AI Agent using Claude Opus 4.7)*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| American Rye | 1.5 oz |
| Disaronno Amaretto | 0.5 oz |
| Cardamom syrup | 0.25 oz |
| Turkish Tobacco bitters | 2 dashes |
| Black Walnut bitters | 1 dash |
| Orange bitters | 1 dash |

**Method:** Stir with ice ~30 seconds. Strain into a chilled coupe, or over a large rock in a rocks glass.

**Garnish:** Cocktail cherry — placed with inexplicable confidence.

**Profile:** Spirit-forward, stirred, brooding whisky cocktail. Cool weather, contemplative. A "thinking person's Manhattan riff."

#### Why it works
Rye spice meets amaretto's almond sweetness — savory fighting sweet. Cardamom syrup bridges them with warmth rather than just sugar. Turkish Tobacco bitters anchor with deep, leathery, slightly sweet smoke. Black walnut adds earthy underpinning. Orange bitters lift the nose so it doesn't get too brooding.

#### Optional theatre
Present to a guest with a confident, slightly wrong description of the ingredients. When corrected, insist you were right and offer to make them another.

### [cocktail2] CardaMama
*Created by Glenn Strycker*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Cognac or Armagnac | 2 oz |
| Cardamom syrup | 0.5 oz |
| Cardamom bitters | 2 dashes |
| Tonic water | top |

**Method:** Shake well with ice. Strain into a coupé glass. Top with chilled tonic water.

**Garnish:** 2 cocktail cherries.

**Profile:** Shaken brandy with effervescence, cardamom-forward, elegant.

#### Why it works
Double-down on cardamom (syrup + bitters) layers the same flavor note rather than muddying with competing elements. Tonic adds lift and just enough bitterness to keep it from going too sweet. Shake-then-top method preserves texture.

### [cocktail3] Wild Sanibel
*Created by Glenn Strycker*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Grapefruit juice | 1/3 of glass |
| Coconut juice (high-quality pink, Whole Foods) | 1/3 of glass |
| Cocchi Italian Vermouth | ~1/6 of glass (float) |
| Cherry bitters | 2 shakes |
| Champagne / Brut sparkling | float to fill |

**Method:** Fill pint glass or highball glass with ice (crushed or large cubes). Add grapefruit juice and coconut juice. Stir to mix thoroughly. Float Cocchi on top. Add cherry bitters. Float champagne to fill.

**Profile:** Refreshing, fruity, bubbly, built-in-glass cocktail. Created for warm-weather, beach-inspired summer drinking.

#### Why it works
The layered float technique creates visual stratification — works exceptionally well as an entertaining drink. Grapefruit + coconut + sparkling + Cocchi balances bright, creamy, dry, and bitter-aromatic.

### [cocktail4] Plum Awesome
*Created by Glenn Strycker*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Reyka Icelandic vodka | 1.25 oz |
| Sweet plum Ume Japanese liqueur | 0.5 oz |
| Sour plum Taiwanese liqueur | 0.5 oz |
| Cocchi Italian Vermouth | 0.5–0.75 oz |
| Cherry bitters | 4 dashes |

**Method:** Shake well with ice. Strain into a chilled glass.

**Garnish:** No citrus or cherry — fresh stone fruit, prune, or dried apricot only.

**Profile:** Plum-forward shaken cocktail. Sweet/sour layered fruit on a neutral vodka base.

#### Why it works
Sweet and sour plum liqueurs in tandem build complexity rather than picking one (same double-layering instinct as CardaMama's cardamom). Cocchi bridges toward something more sophisticated than a straight fruit cocktail. Vodka stays neutral so plum stays front and center. Garnish choice deliberately avoids citrus to keep the stone-fruit profile clean.

### [cocktail5] Dave's Folly
*Built for friend Dave, who likes habanero-infused drinks.*
*Created by Barkeeper Bjorn (Bartender AI Agent using Claude Opus 4.7)*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| American Rye | 1.5 oz |
| Crème de poire | 0.5 oz |
| Fresh lime juice | 0.5 oz |
| Habanero honey syrup | 0.25 oz (start; dial up for heat tolerance) |
| Cherry Maple Smoked bitters | 2 dashes |
| Angostura | 1 dash |

**Method:** Shake hard with ice ~12 seconds. Double-strain into a chilled coupe, or over one large rock.

**Garnish:** Thin habanero ring floated on top (functions as warning label and amuse-bouche). Optional pear slice on rim.

**Profile:** Pear-and-habanero rye cocktail. Spirit-forward with a clear narrative arc: pear → smoke → heat → finish.

#### Why it works
Rye spine gives spice that runs *with* habanero rather than against it. Crème de poire adds a soft fruit sweetness that catches habanero's underlying apricot-floral note (habaneros are fruit-forward — heat is just one variable) and mellows the burn so the drink stays drinkable. Lime brightens. Cherry Maple Smoked bitters meet capsaicin like a barbecue glaze in liquid form. Angostura keeps it from getting dessert-leaning.

#### Variant: Naked Dave (mezcal version)
Sub mezcal 1:1 for the rye. Drop the Angostura. Keep everything else.

#### Habanero honey syrup recipe
- 1/2 cup honey + 1/2 cup water in a small saucepan
- 1 habanero, sliced into rings, seeds in (use 1/2 pepper for milder, both halves for full heat)
- Simmer 5 min, off heat, steep 10–20 min depending on heat target
- Strain, bottle, refrigerate. Keeps ~3 weeks.

Taste at 10 min and pull when it hits "lingering warmth" rather than "fire" — heat builds another notch as it cools.

### [cocktail6] The Crossover
*First citrus-forward smoky original. Mezcal Sidecar with a Cocchi assist.*
*Created by Barkeeper Bjorn (Bartender AI Agent using Claude Opus 4.7)*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mezcal (Montelobos Espadin) | 1.5 oz |
| Cointreau | 0.5 oz |
| Cocchi | 0.5 oz |
| Fresh lemon juice | 0.75 oz |
| Orange bitters | 2 dashes |
| Toasted almond bitters | 1 dash |

**Method:** Shake hard with ice ~12 seconds. Double-strain into a chilled coupe.

**Garnish:** Express a lemon peel over the surface and drop it in.

**Profile:** Bright but layered. Spirit-forward smoke meets citrus. Drinks well in any weather.

#### Why it works
Cointreau carries the sweet load (orange has always flattered agave). Cocchi adds a bitter-aromatic backbone that a straight sour lacks, giving the drink a finish rather than just an end. Toasted almond bitters echo the roasted-agave profile of mezcal. Orange bitters lift the nose so smoke arrives second rather than first. Lemon's higher acidity than lime plays better with smoke — sharper and cleaner.

#### Variation: Sunset Crossover
Swap the 0.5 oz Cocchi for 0.5 oz **Aperol** — slightly sweeter, more sunset-orange in color. Same structural role.

#### Future variations to try
- Swap lemon for grapefruit → Mezcal Hemingway riff
- Add a barspoon of crème de cassis → autumn version

### [cocktail7] Smokey the Pear
*Pear-forward shaken cocktail with a Mexican-desert lean. Iterative — current spec is v2 after a too-smoky/too-sweet v1.*
*Created by Glenn Strycker*

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Mezcal | 1–1.25 oz |
| Second base spirit (white Jamaican rum, gin, or vodka) | 1–1.25 oz |
| Pear juice (Whole Foods carries good options) | 2–2.5 oz |
| Crème de Poire | 0.75 oz |
| Cocchi | 0.5 oz |
| Prickly pear preserves | 1/2 tsp |
| Rhubarb bitters | 3 dashes |
| Angostura | 1 dash |
| Black walnut bitters | 1 dash |

**Method:** Shake hard with ice. Strain into a chilled martini glass.

**Garnish:** Pear slice + cocktail cherry.

**Profile:** Smoky-meets-pear shaken cocktail. Desert/Mexican thematic — the prickly pear preserves are the structural pun (a "pear" that isn't a pear, growing in mezcal country). Recommended sub-spirit pairing: **white Jamaican rum first choice** (funky/vegetal echoes agave's roasted notes; Jamaican esters complement pear esters), **gin second** (London Dry > floral — botanicals can complement pear, but heavy florals will fight it), **vodka third** (subtracts smoke without adding character).

**Image:** <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/strycker/barkeeper-bjorn/refs/heads/user/glennstrycker/images/smokey_the_pear_001.png" width="200"> <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/strycker/barkeeper-bjorn/refs/heads/user/glennstrycker/images/smokey_the_pear_002.png" width="200">

#### Why it works (v2)
The v1 build (1.5 oz mezcal as sole base) put smoke in a fight it couldn't lose — and with three pear sweetness sources stacking (Crème de Poire, pear juice, preserves), the drink swung from too-smoky to too-sweet when diluted. The v2 split-base approach lets the second spirit *carry* the pear rather than asking pear to compete with smoke. Mezcal becomes a flavor accent at ~50% of base rather than the whole structure.

The bitters trio is doing real work: rhubarb echoes mezcal's vegetal underbelly, Angostura provides classic baking-spice anchoring, black walnut adds earthy depth that bridges the desert-fruit theme.

#### v1 (deprecated — for reference only)
Original build was 1.5 oz mezcal as sole base, 1.5 oz pear juice, same other ingredients. Tasted too smoky; diluting with extra pear juice fixed the smoke but made it too sweet and not boozy enough. Lesson: split-base mezcal cocktails generally work better than mezcal-only when pear is the lead flavor — pear's softness gets buried under solo mezcal.

#### Variation 1: Sweeter & mellower (1:1:1:1)
*Best for guests who want the smoke way back.*

Replace the structure with equal pours: **1 oz mezcal + 1 oz Carpano Antica + 1 oz pear juice + 1 oz Crème de Poire**, plus Cocchi as written. (Note: swap Cocchi for Carpano if avoiding two vermouths; or keep both at smaller pours to avoid double-vermouth heaviness.) Optional addition of a small amount of Cointreau is possible but be conservative — orange will overpower pear at anything more than 0.25 oz. Pear jam can substitute for prickly pear preserves to lean further into pure pear.

#### Variation 2: 2x smokier
Add **0.5 oz peaty Scotch** as a float on top of the v2 build. Look at it from a layering standpoint — Scotch hits the nose first, then mezcal smoke comes through mid-palate.

#### Variation 3: 3x smokier
Combine Variation 2 (peaty Scotch float) with a **substitution: cherry smoked bitters in place of (or alongside) the rhubarb bitters**. This pushes the drink fully into smoke-forward territory. Best for cool-weather drinking.

#### Future variations to test
- **Toasted pecan or walnut garnish** — would amplify the desert/Mexican theme
- **Smoked salt rim** — half-rim, optional, ties directly into the smoke layering
- **Aged rum sub** in place of white Jamaican — would add caramelization that could either complement or compete with the prickly pear preserves
